# The Lean library `MassGap`

This is the map of the Lean library in `research/lean/` for readers of the Lean. The paper's section 11 (`research/paper/v3/11-formal.md`) states the trust base, the build and the table of its numbered theorems; the files `research/paper/v3/*.lean-map.md` give the declarations and open hypotheses of every numbered theorem of sections 1 to 9. This file covers the rest of the library: what each part is for, its capstones, its open hypotheses, its modules, and which declarations not to use.

## Conventions

- Every declaration is in the namespace `MassGap.<Module>`, with `<Module>` the file name, except where a file is named. The prefix `MassGap.` is omitted below.
- The Lean coupling is the coefficient of the ordered-plaquette action: `β = β_W/2`.
- Provenance of the one-line statements. A line marked (sig) is read against the Lean signature, as of the paper's lean-map files. Every other line is taken from the module docstring or from the module inventory; its declaration exists by name, and its signature is not read here. Treat unmarked lines as pointers, and read the signature before citing one.
- "Open" means no declaration in the library concludes the statement.
- The module lists are grouped by content. `research/lean/MassGap.lean` imports the modules in build order, not by topic, and its import comments are not a reliable description of the modules.

## The three Clay propositions

Three propositions in the library carry the name "Clay". They are different statements and none implies another.

| Proposition | Couplings | What it states | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ClayStatement` (file `ClayStatementG.lean`), `= ∀ N ≥ 2, ClayStatementG.ClayStatementOf (SU N)` (`ClayStatementG.clayStatement_iff_SU`, `Iff.rfl`) | a measured tower `MeasuredTower.MeasTowerG` | Osterwalder–Schrader axioms OS0 to OS4 on admissible families of separated test functions, for the energy-momentum tensor and all gauge-invariant local composites; a reconstructed Hamiltonian with a gap on the tensor's space and on the composites; asymptotic freedom of the trace two-point kernel; non-Gaussianity; the translation Ward identity; limits along one fixed non-principal ultrafilter. 32 conjuncts in `ClayStatementG.ClayDataP`. | the target; Theorem 1.1 of the paper concludes it |
| `ClayFull` (file `ClayFull.lean`) | the two-loop tower `dyBeta` | for one scalar field (the energy density read through a flow), Schwinger functions on Schwartz test functions, `SO(4)` and translation invariance, OS2 and OS3, a self-adjoint Hamiltonian with a gap, non-Gaussianity, a linked local composite | not used by the paper; no tensor, no composites |
| `ClayRoutes.ClayContinuum`, `ClayRoutes.ClayContinuumPair` | the two-loop tower `dyBeta` | at every dyadic step a gap at physical rate `c/L` of the reconstructed continuum transfer data, and a non-trivial vacuum; no Osterwalder–Schrader axiom in the conclusion | conclusion of the box route (`clay15`) and of the existence-side routes `clay_canonical`, `clay_final`, `clay_two`; the paper's `𝒞(L)` |

The target is `ClayStatement`. Its own docstring lists what it does not say: other groups than `SU(N)` are in `ClayStatementG` (`ClayStatementSO` through `ClayStatementE8`); the Schwinger functions are on separated families, with no extension to coincident points; the limit is along an ultrafilter; the reconstruction to Wightman fields is not formalized; the Weyl channel and composites above dimension four are tied, not normalized. `ClayFull`'s docstring states that `ApertureVolume.clay_two`, which concludes `ClayContinuumPair`, supplies no clause of `ClayFull`.

## Cluster 1. The doubling defect, the bootstrap, the coupling tower and the root

Purpose. The doubling defect of the periodic Wilson torus, `windowCoupling N β w = 16 log Z(2^w) − log Z(2^{w+1})`, read, as an argued interpretation, as a Rényi-2 entropy of the transfer operator. A squaring recursion turns one window with defect at most `1/1000` into vanishing at every larger dyadic torus, and vanishing gives a size-uniform transfer gap. This is a form of the known purity bootstrap (thermal Rényi-2), from OpenAI's two-dimensional O(3) and O(4) work and periodic spin-one Haldane gap proof (github.com/openai/math, commit adc7f12) and Grover's extensions of that proof (arXiv:2611.07400), all cited in the paper; it is not claimed as new. The bridge statement `CouplingTower.StrongEnd N` asks for vanishing at arbitrarily large `β`; the root theorem derives the target from it and two further inputs. This cluster holds the gap half of the argument (paper sections 4 and 5).

## Cluster 2. The anisotropic torus and the strip

Purpose. Embed the isotropic window defect in a two-coupling family, in-plane `β` and mixed `t`. At `t = 0` the torus is exactly `2n²` independent two-dimensional tori, and the two-dimensional defect vanishes at every `β` without a character expansion. The basin squaring spreads vanishing to a strip in `t` of explicit width, and the strip carries a size-uniform transfer gap. The strip stops at `t` of order `1/β` and does not reach the diagonal `t = β`; nothing consumes it toward `StrongEnd` (paper section 7).

Capstones.

- `DecoupledSplit.ZA_decoupled`: `Z(β, 0, n) = Z₂(β, n)^{2n²}` (sig).
- `TwoDVanish.twoD_vanishes`: the two-dimensional defect tends to `0` at every `β ≥ 0`, every `N ≠ 0` (sig). `TwoDVanish.decoupled_vanishes`, `TwoDVanish.weakMixAnchor` (sig).
- `MixAnchor.DA_succ_le`, `MixAnchor.tendsto_DA_of_seed`: the basin on the anisotropic family (sig).
- `StripWidth.strip_width`: vanishing for `0 ≤ t < t_low(β) = e^{−4β}/(16192(16β+27))`, uniform in `N` (sig). `StripWidth.strip_width_poly`, `StripWiden.strip_width_glue` (sig).
- `CumulantStrip.logZA_ext`, `CumulantStrip.DA_le_quartic`, `CumulantStrip.strip_width_quartic`: the quartic cost of the mixed coupling and the widest proved strip (sig).
- `CumulantStrip.dependency_cgf`: cumulant generating function bound on a dependency graph (sig).
- `StripGap.walk_gap`: gap of the plaquette walk (sig). `StripGapThm.strip_gap`, `StripGapThm.strip_cluster`: size-uniform transfer gap on the strip, on cubic tori of side `2^w`, `w ≥ w₀(β)` (sig).

## Cluster 3. The finite-box route

Purpose. A second route to the transfer gap: a Knabe-type criterion turns a local gap on a finite box into a global gap of the heat-bath dynamics, a Lieb–Robinson bound turns that into clustering, and clustering gives the transfer gap of the periodic state. The box local gap is proved at side 15 for `SU(2)` and `SU(3)` at small coupling with explicit constants. The continuum conclusion needs an ultraviolet step that stays open; the one-cube reduction relates the route to the work of Bhattacharjee et al. (paper section 8).

Capstones.

- `KnabeCriterion.knabe_bound` (file `KnabeEngine.lean`): the algebra of Knabe's argument on any module with a non-negative form (sig).

Module names count as live. `research/code/tests/test_doc_cites_live_declarations.py` runs the check as a test and fails when a listed document cites a name the repository does not define, or when a listed document is missing. The check proves that a citation resolves, not that the declaration says what the document says; the (sig) marks above record which statements are read against signatures. It cannot see Mathlib, so a Mathlib lemma no proof here uses would be reported dead; this file cites none.
